## B. PHARMACOVIGILANCE DATA: Adverse Events & Safety Signals (2018-2026)

### Finding: Known Safety Signal (Zuschlag 2018) Remains the Primary Clinical Concern

**Documented Serotonin Syndrome Cases:**
- **Zuschlag 2018 (PMID 30104021):** Systematic review identifying **50 documented cases of methylene blue-induced serotonin syndrome**
  - Combined with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, MAOIs, tramadol, etc.)
  - Presentations ranged from mild (tremor, agitation) to severe (hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, respiratory compromise)
  - Mechanism: Methylene blue acts as monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitor at clinical doses
  - Risk factor clustering: Polypharmacy with serotonergic agents, higher doses (>100 mg/day)

**Post-Zuschlag 2018 Pharmacovigilance (2019-2026):**
- No identifiable NEW major safety signal cluster emerging in literature
- Continued documentation of serotonin toxicity risk in summary articles and reviews
- **Gillman 2011 (PMID 20142303):** Primary mechanistic explanation of serotonergic toxicity (MAO inhibition); remains the authoritative reference

**FDA FAERS Data Search Limitations:**
- FAERS database (accessed via OpenFDA) requires direct querying for "methylene blue" adverse event reports
- No aggregate summary of methylene blue-related FAERS reports (2018-2026) identified in PubMed literature
- Implication: Safety signal data likely exists in raw FAERS database but is not systematically reviewed in published literature

**Poison Control & Toxicology Reports:**
- Poison control centers receive calls involving methylene blue; data not systematically aggregated in peer-reviewed literature
- No major toxicology update papers on methylene blue (2018-2026) identify new adverse event clusters beyond serotonin syndrome

**Hemolysis Risk in G6PD Deficiency:**
- **Youngster 2010 (PMID 20701405):** Evidence-based review documenting methylene blue as risk in G6PD-deficient populations
- Risk applies primarily at higher doses (>100 mg/day, especially IV formulations)
- No new evidence (2018-2026) suggesting increased real-world hemolysis events or expanded risk populations

## E. ADVERSE EVENT CLUSTERING & REAL-WORLD SAFETY CONCERNS (2018-2026)

### Key Finding: Serotonin Syndrome Remains the Dominant Safety Signal; No New Clusters Identified

**Serotonin Syndrome (Zuschlag 2018 Baseline: 50 Cases)**
- Mechanism: Methylene blue's MAO inhibitory properties (monoamine oxidase inhibition at doses ≥1 mg/kg)
- Onset: Typically within hours to days after initiation or dose escalation
- Risk modifiers:
  - Co-medication with serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol)
  - High doses (>100 mg/day, especially IV)
  - Older age, polypharmacy burden
- Severity range: Mild (tremor, agitation) to life-threatening (hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, respiratory failure)

**No Documented Safety Signal Expansion (2019-2026):**
- Literature search yields no reports of epidemic clustering or novel adverse event patterns
- Serotonin syndrome risk remains the primary documented toxicity concern
- Hemolysis in G6PD populations remains secondary concern (lower incidence)

**Other Potential Toxicities NOT Documented in Real-World Clustering:**
- Chloroquine-like retinopathy (theoretical risk, not clinically documented in case series)
- Cardiac arrhythmias (isolated case reports but no clustering)
- Hematologic dyscrasias (limited real-world documentation)

**Implication:**
The real-world adverse event profile of methylene blue remains **relatively static and predictable** based on the Zuschlag 2018 and Gillman 2011 literature. No surprising new toxicity patterns have emerged in peer-reviewed literature (2018-2026).
