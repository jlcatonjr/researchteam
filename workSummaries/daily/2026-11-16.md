## Term register and synced term index (#193)

Operator request, 2026-11-16: every project defines its terms as coded definitions so that consistent
usage can be enforced, every appearance of a listed term is indexed, and the index is kept in sync by a
hook or a similarly reliable trigger. The request asked for a research project first, then a plan, an
audit of the plan, and implementation.

- **Research project.** `Projects/CodedTermIndexing/`: research plan, literature review, analysis,
  conclusion, interpretive map with a Historical setting register, a 69-record bibliography and evidence
  notes. Sources were gathered by three agents, each recording quotations with the address they came
  from. All 36 DOIs were checked against CrossRef.
  - **The 2-fold audit ran in full.** Round 1 found 34 citation and 55 claim problems. Round 2, working
    from the live sources without round 1's notes, confirmed all 101 quotations and raised 16 findings on
    the scholarly sources and 25 tool rows needing a fix.
    A closure check then found 11 round 2 findings and 8 ledger entries only partly closed, and 16 new
    problems; all were remediated. No fabricated or unresolvable source was found; the audit cannot prove
    that none exists.
  - **Two findings changed the argument.** One study had been read as showing that a term register
    failed; its full text calls consistency "fairly high", so it is now cited only as a measurement
    study. A second source turned out to describe a hash test for change, and is now credited for it.
  - **What the project concludes.** The register and the occurrence index have separate histories. The
    nearest precedents for joining them are an identifier dictionary for source code and a specification
    for legal concept records. No documentation tool examined joins definitions, a stored index of
    plain-prose occurrences and a freshness check. The direct evidence that an unchecked register fails
    is one second-hand case, and the conclusion says so.
  - **Not verified, and left uncited:** five ISO standards (the catalogue pages refused every request)
    and three other sources. The ledger lists what a person should still check.
- **Plan and its audit.** `references/plans/term-index-2026-11-16.design.md`. The adversarial verdict on
  the draft was RETHINK: it checked the index's freshness and did not block inconsistent usage, and its
  "unavoidable" gate exists only in this repository's CI. Both were accepted. Fifteen resolutions (R1 to
  R15) are in section 0.
- **Implementation.** Four scripts: `term_index_engine.py` (reads only), `build_term_index.py`,
  `check_term_index.py`, `query_term_index.py`. Bridge commands `term-init`, `term-build`, `term-check`,
  `term-query`; `validate` runs the check. Hook example `.claude/settings.terms.example.json`. Protocol
  `docs/term-index-protocol.md`. Working Rule 8. Registers for all three projects under `Projects/`.
  `term_index_require = true` in `.researchteam`.
- **Reviews of the code.** Five rounds. `@security` gave a conditional pass three times before passing:
  the matcher could be stalled by a crafted register or document, a reused index file could put chosen
  text into the hook's message, and `term-init` could follow a symbolic link. An independent reviewer
  reproduced 15 defects, the worst being a hook run of minutes against a 30-second timeout. All were
  fixed. Every finding and its disposition is in `references/plans/term-index-2026-11-16.audits.md`.
- **Merge.** #193 as `8938f9e`, under PS-2, with `--match-head-commit 144799e`, on green `tests (3.10)`
  and `tests (3.13)`. `@security` PASS at that head naming thirteen paths; `@conflict-auditor` CLEAN at
  that head. Verdicts are in a PR comment.
- **Decisions the session made that the operator may want to change:**
  - A discouraged form outside a quotation, with no recorded exception, **blocks**. The research does
    not settle whether blocking works better than advising; this was a design choice.
  - A register is compulsory for every project **in this repository only**. A derived repository
