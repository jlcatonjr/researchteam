## Executive Summary (200 words)

The official Zelda chronology is most stable when read as a three-branch structure emerging from Ocarina of Time and then interpreted through branch-specific narrative themes rather than sequence alone. The Downfall branch is characterized by long civilizational decline, cyclical catastrophe, and recovery from ruins. The Child branch emphasizes consequences, foreknowledge, and unresolved shadow politics, while the Adult branch foregrounds adaptation after irreversible loss, maritime exploration, and the founding of successor institutions. This thematic partition clarifies why branch debates persist even when game ordering is accepted.

Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom are most plausibly located at extreme chronological distance from the classical split, where prior branch events survive as layered historical memory rather than immediate continuity constraints. This explains mixed legacy signals without requiring over-precise branch pinning unsupported by explicit official statements.

Character mapping supports this approach. Link, Zelda, and Ganon/Ganondorf function as cross-branch archetypal constants, while branch-specific figures such as Midna (Child), Tetra (Adult), and Hilda (Downfall) preserve branch identity. Tingle is an important test case: highly visible in Child and Adult contexts, but not a reliable all-branch anchor. Overall, the branch model remains robust, and BOTW/TOTK are best interpreted as intentionally distant horizon entries.
