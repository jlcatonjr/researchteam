# Research Plan: Early History and Influence of Labor Matching Models in Macroeconomics

Prepared by: @topic-scoping-expert  
Date: April 18, 2026  
Output directory: Projects/Matching/LaborMatchingMacroeconomics/

## Research Question

How did labor matching models become a dominant framework in modern macroeconomics, and what are the central theoretical tensions between individual/submarket equilibrium conditions and full aggregate general equilibrium consistency?

## Scope

In scope:
- Early intellectual origins of labor market search and matching (1960s-1980s)
- Consolidation of the Diamond-Mortensen-Pissarides (DMP) framework
- Directed search and submarket equilibrium approaches
- Integration of matching models into quantitative macroeconomics
- Core debates over unemployment fluctuations, wage determination, and efficiency

Out of scope:
- Full technical derivations of all model variants
- Country-specific institutional labor market case studies
- Reduced-form empirical literatures not directly linked to equilibrium search/matching structure

## Method and Selection Criteria

1. Prioritize field-defining and widely cited journal articles and monographs.
2. Use representative coverage across foundational theory, equilibrium efficiency conditions, and macro calibration/quantitative debates.
3. Distinguish clearly between:
- Individual optimization and local/submarket equilibrium
- Aggregate consistency and macro general equilibrium closure
4. Present disagreements in the literature as disagreements about mechanisms, assumptions, and equilibrium concepts.

## Key Analytical Axes

1. Frictional coordination and matching technology:
- Matching function as reduced-form equilibrium object
- Congestion, search externalities, and market tightness

2. Wage setting and propagation:
- Nash bargaining benchmark
- Alternative wage-setting rules and cyclicality

3. Equilibrium granularity:
- Random search with one aggregate market tightness
- Directed search with submarket posting and composition effects

4. Macro closure and amplification:
- Partial-equilibrium intuition versus full GE constraints
- Role of capital, productivity shocks, and policy wedges
